Two people want to agree on a key over a channel that may be tapped. The **BB84** protocol, by Bennett and Brassard (1984), uses quantum mechanics so that eavesdropping leaves traces.

## The idea

Alice encodes each bit in one of two bases, chosen at random:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 & \text{basis } Z & \text{basis } X \\ \hline
\text{bit } 0 & \ket{0} & \ket{+} \\
\text{bit } 1 & \ket{1} & \ket{-}
\end{array}
$$

Whoever measures in the right basis reads the bit with certainty. Whoever measures in the wrong basis gets a random result **and** changes the state.

Alice sends $\ket{+}$ and Bob measures in the Z basis. What is the probability that he gets $0$?

```exercise:number
id: bb84-base-errada
answer: 0.5
tolerance: 0.001
```

## The protocol

1. Alice picks random bits and bases and sends the qubits.
2. Bob measures each one in a random basis.
3. In public, they compare the **bases** (never the bits) and discard the cases where they differ. This is called **sifting**.
4. They reveal part of the remaining bits to estimate the error rate.
5. If the rate is low, they correct errors, reduce the spy's information and use the rest as the key.

On average, how many bits remain after sifting if Alice sends **1000** qubits?

```exercise:number
id: bb84-peneiramento
answer: 500
```

## When there is eavesdropping

Suppose Eve intercepts every qubit, measures each one in a random basis and resends what she saw. In the bits that remain after sifting:

- with probability $\tfrac12$, she picks the right basis and goes unnoticed;
- with probability $\tfrac12$, she picks the wrong basis and sends a wrong state, and then Bob gets the bit wrong half of the time.

What error rate does Eve cause in the sifted bits?

```exercise:number
id: bb84-erro
answer: 0.25
tolerance: 0.001
```

If Alice and Bob compare $n$ of these bits, the chance that Eve escapes without causing any error is $(3/4)^n$.

With $n = 10$, what is the probability that Eve is **not** noticed? (Three decimal places.)

```exercise:number
id: bb84-deteccao
answer: 0.0563
tolerance: 0.001
```

The security comes from no-cloning: if Eve could copy the qubits, she would keep the copies and measure them later, already knowing the bases. The classical channel must be **authenticated**; otherwise, Eve could pretend to be Bob.
